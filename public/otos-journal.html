Your process, at a glance

Remember the third rule: judge the decision, not the outcome. Over any short run, luck drowns out skill, so the number that matters most here is how often you followed your process, not your win rate.

RULE 1

Survive first

No single trade or run of trades may threaten your ability to keep trading. You cannot recover from zero.

RULE 2

No trade is always allowed

Doing nothing is a complete, professional decision, and usually the right one. Make a habit of comfortably saying no.

RULE 3

Judge the decision, not the outcome

A win from a broken process is a bad decision that got lucky. Grade yourself on whether you followed the process.

Ready to log a decision?

Every decision counts, including the no-trades. They often teach you the most.

Daily prep, Check Yourself

Before you look at a single chart, read yourself honestly, the same way you read the market. Decide your limits now, in a calm moment, so they protect you later when your judgement is at its worst.

Week without trading (practice mode)

The habit the guide prescribes: for a week, run the full checklist on live charts but place no trades at all. The one goal is to get completely comfortable reaching a no. While this is on, new decisions default to no-trade and the dashboard tracks your run.

Weekly review, two separate loops

Once a week, separate two very different questions and never let them blur. Loop one: are my rules good? (Change a rule only on a sample of trades, never after a single loss.) Loop two: did I follow my rules well? (Fix that with discipline and practice, not by rewriting the rules.) The two problems have opposite cures.

The OTOS method, keep it beside you

You are not trying to predict the market. You are running a process that only lets good, survivable trades through, and calmly says no to everything else.

The seven stages (the funnel)
1
Read the world. Describe what the market is doing, plainly and with no opinion attached.
2
Check yourself. Know your remaining risk and your honest state before you look for setups.
3
The green light gates. Pass all three, permission, then tradability, then opportunity, or stop.
4
Choose the play. Match today's conditions to a written play, or accept the no-trade.
5
How sure, how much. Grade conviction, then size so that being wrong barely hurts you.
6
Commit and set your line. Write your thesis and your invalidation, then place the order as planned.
7
Review and improve. Log every decision, and fix your rules and your execution separately.
The complete decision checklist

Run this before every trade, top to bottom. The moment you reach a no, or an honest not sure, you stop, and that is a no-trade. Every box must be a confident yes to continue.

Can I trade at all? inside my limits, clear of any window I avoid, genuinely clear headed.
Can I describe this market plainly? its character, movement, and where price sits, no opinion mixed in.
Is this environment worth engaging? a market I trade well, not one I'm forcing out of boredom.
Is there real opportunity? genuine room for reward against the risk, not merely a signal that fired.
Does a play I trust actually fit? today's conditions match a written play without bending or excusing.
Do I know my line? I can state exactly what proves me wrong, and precisely where that line sits.
Is my size survivable? if I'm wrong and get stopped, the loss barely scratches my account.
Will I log it either way? ready to record this decision and its reasons, win or lose.
The default is no trade. If you cannot honestly tick every box, the professional move is to wait. Waiting is the job.
Describe, then decide

Describe means observe and state what is true about the market and yourself, with no opinion attached. Decide means choose an action based on those descriptions. Stages 1-3 are mostly describing; the later stages are deciding. When you let a description quietly become a decision, a nice-looking candle instantly becomes buying, you have skipped the entire process. See clearly first, choose deliberately second.

The professional order: you do not bring a strategy to the market. You read the market, and the market hands you the strategy. If no play fits the conditions in front of you, you do not trade, and that is a win, not a missed opportunity.

Glossary of key terms
OTOS. The Orca Trading Operating System, the decision process you run before risking money.
The funnel. The ordered stages every possible trade must pass; each stage can only pass or stop.
Gate. A pass/stop check that can end the process. Each gate is a veto.
No-trade default. Doing nothing is the starting answer; you only trade when every check clearly says yes.
Tradability. Whether the current market is the kind of environment you personally trade well.
Opportunity. Genuine room for reward relative to the risk, not the same thing as a signal.
Conviction. How strong a setup is, graded on a simple scale you define and apply the same way each time.
Your line (invalidation). The point, decided before you enter, that proves your trade idea wrong.
Survivable size. A position small enough that being wrong barely affects your ability to keep trading.
Process vs outcome. Judging a trade by whether you followed a sound process, not by whether it won.
The two review loops. Checking separately whether your rules are good, and whether you followed them well.
Signal. An event that says something happened; worth trading only if it also carries real opportunity.
